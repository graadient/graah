The arms, decoded

Three red six-pointed stars — read them as the three kingdoms the family has called home: Denmark, Norway, Sweden. The blue-gold-red arc is a ready-made Bifröst, the Norse bridge between worlds.

The name in runes

GRAAH in Younger Futhark — the Viking-age alphabet of Denmark and Norway. Five strokes of carved geometry: kaun, reið, ár, ár, hagall.

One star, two meanings

The rune ᚼ hagall is the H that ends GRAAH — and it is drawn as a six-armed star. It is the heraldic star rebuilt from three straight lines. That is the mark.

Direction B · Light neoclassical

Gustavian

Swedish and Danish neoclassicism — limestone, grey-blue panelling, gilt hairlines, Thorvaldsen's marble. Bodoni is from exactly that era (1790s), so the type is period-true. The Greek key border becomes a band of runes. Calm, literate, built to last a century.

Rule 1 — the triad is earned

Blue, gold and red come straight from the arms. Use them small: arcs, stars, a link, one stat. Never as big flat blocks like the current template.

Rule 2 — runes as texture

Runes go in borders, seals and the wordmark's shadow line, never in running text. One alphabet: Younger Futhark. ᚴᚱᛅᛅᚼ is the only spelling.

Rule 3 — one star

The hagall star ᚼ is the everyday mark: favicon, bullet, cursor, loader. The full shield is for ceremony: hero, seal, print.
